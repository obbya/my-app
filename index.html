    How the numbers work
    
        
      
          
    1. Taxes come out first, using 2026 federal brackets and the standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly), Social Security at 6.2% up to $184,500 of wages per earner, Medicare at 1.45%, and the extra 0.9% Medicare tax above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (joint). Health, dental and vision premiums are pre-tax, so they lower every one of those taxes.
      2. 
          
    2. State tax is a flat rate after the per-person exemption. Michigan is 4.25%; the $5,800 exemption is the 2025 figure, so edit it if your 2026 withholding tables differ. Other states are simplified flat-rate estimates.
      3. 
          
    3. Loan, credit card and pet payments are fixed. Then savings is paid first: 15% of gross for standard incomes, rising to 20% at $200,000 and toward 25% for very high earners.
      4. 
          
    4. What’s left is split across the spending categories by golden-rule weights, never past each cap (housing stays at or under 28% of gross and 35% of take-home).
      5. 
          
    5. Extra (long-term aspirations) receives any leftover, so all 55 line items always add up to gross pay to the cent.
      6. 
        
    
        
    Retirement savings here come out of take-home pay. A pre-tax 401(k) would lower taxes further. These are classroom estimates, not tax advice.
